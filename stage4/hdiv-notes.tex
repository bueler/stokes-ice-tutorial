\begin{center}
{\large\bf H(div)--SIP Stokes on extruded ice-sheet meshes:\ where coercivity fails}\\[1pt]
{\small stage4/ on branch \texttt{margin-column-hdiv}, November 2026}
\end{center}

\textbf{Setup.} \texttt{solve.py -hdiv} uses RTCF2\,$\times$\,DQ1 (the $\mathrm{RT}_1/\mathbb{Q}_1$ pair of [CKS]), so $\nabla\cdot u_h=0$ exactly, with symmetric interior penalty (SIP) as in [CKS, (2.11)] but with $2\nu D(u)$:
\[
\textstyle -\int_{\mathcal{F}} \avgb{2\nu D(u)}:\jump{v\otimes n} - \int_{\mathcal{F}} \avgb{2\nu D(v)}:\jump{u\otimes n} + \int_{\mathcal{F}} \kappa\,\avgb{2\nu}\,\jump{u\otimes n}:\jump{v\otimes n},
\]
plus the one-sided (Nitsche) version on the bed. Degenerate columns, where the Piola map is singular, are excluded:\ a column is a \emph{margin column} if $\min s<\max(h_{tol},\theta\max s)$ over its base-mesh vertices ($\theta=0.3$), else a \emph{thick column}. Stokes is posed only on thick columns; margin-column dofs are pinched, and facets between thick and margin columns get hydrostatic normal stress $p_h=\rho g(s-z)$. Mass leaving thick columns enters margin columns through the SKE source (conservative; compare [B21] on why margins limit local conservation anyway).

\textbf{Geometric penalty.} With column taper $\tau=t_{\min}/t_{\text{avg}}$ and shear factor $S=m_z(s_{\max}-s_{\min})/s_{\min}$ (layer slope $\times$ cell width / cell height), a trace-inequality estimate gives
$\kappa_F=\alpha\,|F|\max_{K=K^\pm}(1+S_K^2)/(|K|\,\tau_K)$.
At $k=0$ (first time step) on 9 meshes $\times$ $\alpha\in\{3,5,10\}$:\ $\tau$ alone $\to$ 19/27 converge; $\tau$ and $S$ $\to$ 24/27. All failures are at $m_x=50$ with large $m_z$; horizontal refinement is harmless. Q2\,$\times$\,DQ1 converges in 9--15 Newton iterations on the same meshes, so the nonlinear problem itself is not hard.

\textbf{Conclusions.} (i) Indefiniteness of the discrete SIP form and solver failure go together (3 meshes). (ii) The geometric $\kappa$ handles sheared margin cells; the remaining indefiniteness is where we were not looking:\ the very stiff ice at the bed under the divide. There $D(u)\to0$, so $\nu$ rises to its regularized ceiling $\tfrac12 B_3(\epsilon D_{\text{typ}})^{-2/3}\approx1.6\times10^{15}$\,Pa\,s ($\epsilon=10^{-4}$), versus $10^{13}$--$10^{14}$ nearby, in flat cells (42\,m\,$\times$\,600\,m at $m_z=24$). Coercivity of SIP requires $\kappa$ to dominate $\nu$-weighted trace terms, so it needs roughly $\kappa\gtrsim C_{tr}\,\nu_{\max}/\nu_{\min}$ near each facet; a fixed $\alpha$ with the arithmetic mean $\avgb{2\nu}$ does not provide this.

\textbf{Next:\ viscosity-weighted averages [ESZ].}
\begin{itemize}
\item Consistency terms:\ replace $\avgb{2\nu D(u)}$ by $\omega^+(2\nu D(u))^+ + \omega^-(2\nu D(u))^-$ with $\omega^\pm=\nu^\mp/(\nu^++\nu^-)$ (weight toward the softer side).
\item Penalty:\ replace $\avgb{2\nu}$ by the harmonic mean $2\nu_h$, $\nu_h=2\nu^+\nu^-/(\nu^++\nu^-)$. This makes coercivity robust to $\nu$ \emph{jumps across} facets.
\item Contrast \emph{within} a cell (as under the divide) is not covered:\ add a factor $\nu_{\max,K}/\nu_{\min,K}$ to $\kappa$, estimated from quadrature-point values.
\item Related levers:\ larger $\epsilon$ lowers the viscosity ceiling, hence the contrast (a physical, not numerical, choice). For Newton, the power-law tangent viscosity is $2\nu/n$ in the $D(u)$ direction, so the Jacobian may need about $n$ times more penalty than the frozen-$\nu$ form.
\end{itemize}
